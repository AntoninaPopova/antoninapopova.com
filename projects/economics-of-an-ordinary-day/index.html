FINAL REFLECTION BEYOND THE NUMBERS

Equal time.
Unequal use.

We all have the same number of hours in a day, but we do not necessarily have the same freedom to decide how to spend them.

What appears to be a small difference in the allocation of time can, when repeated over years, become a substantial difference in economic opportunities. It can influence the time available for education, professional development, paid employment, rest or personal interests.

Unpaid work is an essential part of the functioning of our economies and societies. Yet much of it remains outside conventional measures of economic activity.

Putting a monetary value on this work does not capture its full importance. Nor does it imply that every unpaid hour should become a paid one. But it offers another perspective on something that is easily overlooked in everyday life: the economic significance of how our time is distributed.

The economics of an ordinary day extends far beyond the day itself.
